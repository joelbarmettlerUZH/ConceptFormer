\begin{abstract}
  We study \emph{knowledge injection} into frozen large language models: encoding a
  knowledge-graph entity's 1-hop neighborhood into $k$ continuous ``concept tokens'' that are
  spliced into the frozen model's input, replacing $\sim$100 tokens of retrieved text with
  $k\!\approx\!8$. The encoder --- a small Perceiver-style resampler over the frozen model's own
  label embeddings --- is trained \emph{label-free} by full-vocabulary KL self-distillation
  against the same frozen model reading the facts as text. Under a hardened evaluation protocol
  (full 14{,}266-question \popqa{}, leakage-free held-out splits, paired statistics), we find:
  (i) concept tokens beat question-aware text retrieval and LLM-written summaries by
  $2.6\text{--}3.2\times$ at an 8-token budget, and an untrained-injection control is near floor,
  so the learned compression carries the effect; (ii) unseen-entity accuracy \emph{scales with
  training data} ($2.05\times$ from 10k to 100k training entities, not yet saturated) but
  \emph{anti-scales with frozen-model size at fixed data} (the injection margin roughly halves
  from 0.8B to 2B parameters); (iii) counterfactual edge-swap probes show the model genuinely
  \emph{reads} the injected graph, increasingly so with $k$; and (iv) the same encoder can inject
  through a multimodal model's \emph{vision-token} interface at parity with the text interface at
  \ktok{8}. The two scaling trends pull in opposite directions, making the data$\times$model-size
  interaction the central open question; we lay out the measurement program (up to 1M entities,
  0.8B--397B backbones) designed to resolve it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large language models access factual knowledge through two established channels. Knowledge stored
\emph{in the weights} is uneditable, expensive to refresh, and unreliably recalled for long-tail
entities --- recent evidence suggests frontier models \emph{encode} 95--98\% of long-tail facts
but successfully \emph{recall} only 25--33\% of them \citep{emptyshelves}. Knowledge provided
\emph{as retrieved text} is faithful and editable but costs hundreds of context tokens per query,
paid again on every request. A third channel is emerging in the literature: compressing knowledge
into a few \emph{continuous tokens} consumed directly by a frozen model
\citep{xrag,icae,pisco,kblam}.

ConceptFormer operates in this third channel with a specific commitment: the unit of compression
is a \emph{knowledge-graph entity}. For each Wikidata entity we encode its 1-hop neighborhood ---
an unordered set of (property, neighbor) pairs --- into $k$ soft ``concept tokens'' that are
spliced into the input embeddings of a \emph{frozen} LLM. The encoder is amortized and inductive:
an unseen entity requires one forward pass, no per-entity training. Training requires no labeled
QA data; the student (frozen LLM reading $k$ concept tokens) is distilled against the teacher (the
\emph{same} frozen LLM reading the facts as text) by full-vocabulary KL along the teacher's greedy
answer path. A first version of this idea \citep{cfv1} was validated on GPT-2 with rank-based
metrics; this project rebuilds it on modern backbones (Qwen3-0.6B; Qwen3.5-0.8B/2B) with a
generative exact-match protocol, and asks the questions v1 could not: does the encoder
\emph{generalize} to unseen entities, does the model actually \emph{read} the injected graph, and
how does all of this \emph{scale}?

This report consolidates roughly three months of experiments (about 60 training runs and a
systematic evaluation overhaul) for new collaborators. It reads like a paper because most of it
will become one, but we flag throughout which claims are established, which are single-pilot
signals, and which experiments are planned but not yet run.

\paragraph{Summary of what we believe we know.} (C1) At matched token budgets $\le$32, concept
tokens dominate every text baseline we could construct, including question-aware retrieval
(\S\ref{sec:efficiency}). (C2) The effect is the \emph{trained encoder}: untrained injection of
the same embeddings is near floor. (C3) Unseen-entity generalization scales with training
entities and had not saturated at 100k (\S\ref{sec:datascaling}). (C4) At fixed training data,
the injection margin \emph{shrinks} as the frozen backbone grows (\S\ref{sec:modelscaling}).
(C5) Counterfactual interventions show genuine, capacity-limited graph reading
(\S\ref{sec:faithfulness}). (C6) Injection preserves the frozen model's behavior off-topic.
(C7) The recipe transfers across model families and attention architectures without retuning,
and can enter through a vision-token interface at parity for small $k$ (\S\ref{sec:port}).
(C8) At our evaluation scale, most single-run effect sizes below $\sim$4 points are measurement
noise unless item-paired statistics are used; our corrected protocol (\S\ref{sec:protocol}) is
itself a reusable contribution.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Soft-token context compression.} A line of work compresses \emph{text} into few
continuous tokens: gist tokens \citep{gist} and AutoCompressor \citep{autocompressor} adapt the
LM itself; ICAE \citep{icae} and 500xCompressor \citep{fivehundredx} train LoRA encoders against
frozen decoders with reconstruction objectives; PISCO \citep{pisco} uses sequence-level
distillation but reports that frozen decoders underperform (their Appendix~G) --- a claim our
frozen-decoder results contradict at entity granularity; CompLLM \citep{compllm} distills
per-segment ``concept embeddings'' via activation matching; ARC-Encoder \citep{arcencoder} is the
strongest recent frozen-decoder text compressor. Closest to us is xRAG \citep{xrag}, which shares
our frozen decoder, input-embedding injection, and same-model KL self-distillation --- but xRAG
\emph{translates} a single pre-existing retriever vector rather than learning compression, is
retriever-bound at inference, and evaluates neither unseen-entity generalization nor faithfulness.
Cartridges \citep{cartridges} shares the distillation objective family but trains a KV cache
\emph{per corpus} by gradient descent: no amortization, no generalization to unseen items.
None of these works compresses \emph{structured} input, and none runs causal probes on the
compressed representation; the only faithfulness-adjacent work is post-hoc overflow probing
\citep{overflow}.

\paragraph{Knowledge-graph injection into LLMs.} GNP \citep{gnp}, GraphToken \citep{graphtoken},
G-Retriever \citep{gretriever}, and LLaGA \citep{llaga} encode graphs into soft prompts for
frozen LLMs, but all encode \emph{per question} with task cross-entropy on labeled QA data, and
none precomputes reusable per-entity tokens. KBLaM \citep{kblam} precomputes one key--value pair
\emph{per triple} injected into every attention layer via modified attention --- no neighborhood
aggregation, synthetic-KB evaluation only. Knowledge Prompts \citep{knowledgeprompts} is the
closest precompute precedent: per-entity soft prompts for 1.1M Wikidata entities --- but as a
lookup table of free parameters, so unseen entities are impossible by construction. KAPING
\citep{kaping} is the ``facts as text'' baseline family we compare against. Recent meta-analyses
find that graph-token LLMs ``do not fully understand graph tokens'' \citep{gteval} and that graph
tokens can degenerate into attention sinks decoupled from graph semantics \citep{sink} ---
diagnoses that motivate exactly the counterfactual evaluation we build in
(\S\ref{sec:faithfulness}).

\paragraph{Positioning.} No prior work combines: an \emph{amortized, inductive} encoder producing
$k$ \emph{query-independent} soft tokens per KG entity; \emph{label-free same-model} full-vocab
distillation; \emph{unseen-entity} and \emph{causal-faithfulness} evaluation; at 100k+ entity
scale. Each neighbor has some of these properties; none has the conjunction. On the scaling side,
knowledge-capacity laws exist for \emph{parametric} storage \citep{physics33,memorylayers}, and
prompt-tuning quality is known to improve with model scale for \emph{tasks} \citep{lester}, but
no study measures injection quality against frozen-model scale within one family, and no
data-scaling law for a knowledge encoder exists. Those two empty cells are this project's target.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Setup.} Let $M$ be a frozen chat LLM with input-embedding dimension $d$. For an entity
$e$ with 1-hop neighborhood $G_e=\{(p_i, n_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ (property and neighbor labels from
Wikidata, rank-ordered by PageRank), the \emph{featurizer} embeds each edge as
$x_i = [\,\phi(p_i)\,;\,\phi(n_i)\,] \in \mathbb{R}^{2d}$, where $\phi$ mean-pools $M$'s own
input embeddings over the label's tokens. Features are thus inductive (any labeled entity works)
and live in a space $M$ already understands.

\paragraph{Encoder.} A Perceiver/Flamingo-style latent-query resampler
\citep{perceiver,flamingo}: $k$ learned latent queries cross-attend over the edge set (masked,
permutation-invariant), self-attend, and pass through a feed-forward block, for $L$ layers
(default $d_{\text{model}}{=}1024$, $L{=}4$, $\sim$70M parameters); an output projection maps to
$\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}$. The output projection is \emph{zero-initialized}, so at step~0 the
student is bit-identical to the frozen model --- capability preservation by construction (we
found this variant, ``gate-none'', also more stable than a multiplicative tanh gate).

\paragraph{Objective: same-model KL self-distillation.} The teacher is $M$ reading the facts as
text: $[\text{system}][\text{verbalized } G_e][\text{question}]$, from which we store the greedy
answer path $y_{1..m}$ offline. The student is $M$ reading
$[\text{system}][C_k(G_e)][\text{question}]$, where $C_k$ is the encoder output spliced into the
input embeddings at a contiguous, unit-step RoPE span. The loss is the token-level
full-vocabulary forward KL along the stored path,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} \;=\; \frac{\tau^2}{m}\sum_{t=1}^{m}
  \mathrm{KL}\!\left(\, p_M(\cdot \mid \text{facts}, q, y_{<t}) \;\|\;
                        p_M(\cdot \mid C_k(G_e), q, y_{<t}) \,\right),
\end{equation}
with temperature $\tau{=}1$. No QA labels are used anywhere in training; the corpus questions
(generated by a separate LLM from the graphs; see the data pipeline below) exist only to give
the teacher something to answer. Only the $\sim$20 path positions are materialized through the LM head, which makes
training memory-light. The verbalized teacher context is answer-guaranteed (the questioned edge is
always present), which at our 2048-token budget changes almost nothing since a median neighborhood
verbalizes to $\sim$100 tokens.

\paragraph{Injection ports.} By default the concept block enters through the \emph{text port}:
spliced directly into the input-embedding stream at the position where the facts text sat for the
teacher. For natively multimodal backbones (every Qwen3.5 size) we additionally implemented a
\emph{vision port}: the same $k$ vectors enter as a pseudo-image ---
$[\texttt{vision\_start}][k\times\texttt{image\_token}][\texttt{vision\_end}]$ token ids, with
the model's own M-RoPE grid positions for a $1\times k$ patch layout, exactly as a real
$k$-patch image would. The hypothesis: multimodal models are \emph{pretrained} to consume
continuous out-of-vocabulary token streams at these positions, which may make the vision
interface a better ``landing pad'' for soft tokens than a text stream where every pretraining
token was a row of the embedding matrix.

\paragraph{Data pipeline.} From the Wikidata PageRank dump we sample entity pools
(\popqa{} subjects excluded), snapshot complete 1-hop neighborhoods, generate QA with an
independent LLM (Gemma via vLLM), \emph{tier} each question by whether the graph demonstrably
helps the backbone (base fails, facts-in-context succeeds), and store the teacher's greedy path.
Corpora in play: 10k entities ($92{,}177$ distill rows under the Qwen3-0.6B teacher; $84{,}843$
under Qwen3.5-0.8B --- a stronger base answers more parametrically, so its tiered corpus is
smaller and harder), 100k entities ($925{,}178$ rows), a built 260k-entity snapshot, and a
planned 1M. Note the consequence: \emph{the corpus is teacher-relative}, so cross-backbone
comparisons are of ``each model with its own curriculum'', not of identical training sets.

\section{Evaluation protocol (and the pitfalls it fixes)}
\label{sec:protocol}

Every result is reported against two brackets from the same frozen model: \textbf{base} (no
knowledge; floor) and \textbf{RAG} (facts as text, answer-guaranteed; ceiling, $\approx$0.94--0.96
on full \popqa{}). Metric: greedy exact-match with alias matching (we also report the official,
more lenient \popqa{} metric; it tracks ours within $\sim$0.5\,pt). Three accuracy axes:
\textbf{held-out} (unseen questions about \emph{trained} entities), \textbf{held-in} (trained
questions; overfitting gauge), and \textbf{\popqa{}} (unseen \emph{entities}; the honest headline,
since the encoder must generalize inductively).

An external review mid-project identified four measurement defects that our earlier numbers
carried; all are fixed, and all headline numbers in this report come from the corrected protocol.
We document them because they are easy to reproduce in any similar project:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Seed-coupled eval sampling.} Evaluation subsets were sampled with the training
  seed, so every run scored \emph{different} $n{=}200$ questions; cross-seed spread conflated
  model variance with $\pm$3.5\,pt binomial sampling noise. Fix: one frozen, documented eval seed
  for everything; smaller samples are prefixes of larger ones, so any two runs pair item-by-item.
  \item \textbf{Tiny benchmark subsets.} $n{=}200$ of 14{,}267 \popqa{} questions. Fix: evaluate
  the \emph{full} benchmark (binomial noise $\sim$0.4\,pt); report Wilson intervals and exact
  McNemar tests on shared items. After this fix, apparent seed-to-seed standard deviations
  collapsed from 4--8\,pt to 0.2--2.4\,pt: most of what we had been calling run variance was
  measurement noise.
  \item \textbf{Selection bias.} Best-checkpoint selection used the same $n{=}200$ sample later
  reported. Fix: definitive re-scoring on larger, disjointly-sampled sets.
  \item \textbf{Paraphrase leakage.} Splitting by \emph{question} let paraphrases of the same
  (entity, fact) straddle train/held-out --- measured at \textbf{32.8\%} of held-out rows. Fix:
  split by fact; filter legacy splits at eval time. Held-out numbers dropped 2--4\,pt everywhere;
  orderings survived.
\end{enumerate}
Additionally, the token-efficiency baseline was strengthened from a single query-independent
truncation to three retrieval modes (\S\ref{sec:efficiency}) to pre-empt the strawman objection,
and an untrained-injection control isolates the encoder's contribution.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Token efficiency: concept tokens vs.\ text at matched budgets}
\label{sec:efficiency}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/token_efficiency.png}
  \caption{Accuracy vs.\ knowledge tokens spent at inference (log scale), frozen Qwen3-0.6B,
  100k-entity corpus. Concept tokens (blue, mean$\pm$std over 3 seeds) dominate all three text
  baselines below $\sim$32 tokens on both axes; text catches up only at 64--128 tokens. Left:
  strict held-out; right: full \popqa{} (unseen entities).}
  \label{fig:efficiency}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:efficiency} shows the central comparison. At an 8-token budget on unseen
entities, concept tokens reach $0.477$ where the \emph{best} text baseline (an LLM-written
budgeted summary) reaches $0.147$, and question-aware retrieval --- which may select facts
\emph{after seeing the question}, an advantage our query-independent tokens do not have ---
reaches $0.073$; at 16 tokens the gaps remain $2\times$ or larger. Text needs 64--128 tokens
(most of a verbalized neighborhood; median 100 tokens) to catch up, i.e., matched accuracy costs
text $\sim$5--6$\times$ more tokens. Two controls make this meaningful. First, the
\textbf{untrained-injection control}: filling the same $k$ slots with mean-pooled embeddings of
the top-$k$ edges scores $0.130$ on \popqa{} (base $0.103$), identically for $k{=}8$ and
$k{=}16$ --- the learned compression carries $\sim$93\% of the injected-knowledge effect.
Second, the corrected $k$-curve (Table~\ref{tab:kcurve}) rises monotonically through $k{=}32$
with every adjacent step significant under paired McNemar (e.g.\ $k16{\to}k32$:
$p\!\approx\!10^{-38}$) --- our earlier belief in a ``knee at $k{=}16$'' was an $n{=}200$
artifact. Returns per token diminish: $k{=}8$ buys $\sim$89\% of $k{=}32$'s \popqa{} at a quarter
of the tokens.

\subsection{Data scaling: more entities, better generalization}
\label{sec:datascaling}

Scaling the training corpus from 10k to 100k entities (identical eval sets, $k{=}8$, Qwen3-0.6B)
lifts full-\popqa{} accuracy from $0.232\pm0.011$ to $0.477\pm0.002$ --- a $2.05\times$ gain in
unseen-entity generalization, still climbing at the end of training. Under the strict protocol,
held-out \emph{also} rises ($0.453 \to 0.545$); our earlier belief that held-out was flat across
scales was a leakage artifact (the 25-epoch 10k models overfit paraphrases more). Meanwhile
held-in \emph{fell} ($\sim$0.82 $\to \sim$0.64): the model memorizes training entities less and
learns the edge$\to$concept mapping more --- the signature of graph-learning rather than corpus
memorization. In margin terms, the concept-over-base margin on unseen entities grows from
$+12.9$ to $+37.4$ points with $10\times$ data.

\subsection{Model scaling at fixed data: the margin anti-scales}
\label{sec:modelscaling}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/pilot_scaling_surface.png}
  \caption{The pilot scaling surface. Left: at fixed training data (10k entities), the
  \popqa{} injection margin over each backbone's own base \emph{shrinks} as the frozen model
  grows (0.6B is the Qwen3 anchor; 0.8B/2B are Qwen3.5, 2 seeds each). Right: at a fixed
  backbone (0.6B), the margin \emph{grows} with training entities. The two axes pull in opposite
  directions; their interaction is unmeasured and is the central open question.}
  \label{fig:surface}
\end{figure}

To probe the model axis we migrated to the Qwen3.5 family (natively multimodal at every size)
and ran a two-point transect at fixed 10k-entity data: 0.8B and 2B backbones, $k\in\{8,16\}$,
two seeds each, full-benchmark evaluation (Figure~\ref{fig:surface}, left). Each backbone gets
its own teacher-relative corpus and brackets. The result: the \popqa{} margin over base falls
from $+12.9$\,pt (0.6B anchor) to $+9.4$ (0.8B) to $+5.1$ (2B) at $k{=}8$, and roughly halves
from 0.8B to 2B at $k{=}16$ ($+15.1 \to +6.5$) --- while strict held-out stays healthy at 2B
(0.465--0.503), so the encoder still works; what shrinks is its \emph{marginal value on unseen
entities}. Two interpretations are live and the data cannot yet separate them: a \emph{ceiling
effect} (larger bases know more of \popqa{}: base rises $0.103 \to 0.124 \to 0.150$) versus a
\emph{steerability effect} (a larger frozen model needs more training signal per unit of
behavioral change from $k$ soft tokens). The 100k-corpus corners of the planned surface
(\S\ref{sec:open}) are designed to disentangle them. Migration itself was uneventful: the locked
recipe (optimizer, learning rate, batch, capacity) transferred across families and across an
attention-architecture change (Qwen3.5 is a hybrid linear-attention model) with zero retuning
--- and, notably, the two-point transect already shows the model axis is \emph{not} a free win,
which is precisely why it needs to be measured rather than assumed.

\subsection{Injection port: text stream vs.\ vision stream}
\label{sec:port}

On the same pilots we compared the text port against the vision port (item-paired, same frozen
eval sets). At $k{=}8$ the ports are at \textbf{parity} on \popqa{} at both sizes (deltas
$-0.9$/$+2.4$\,pt at 0.8B, $-0.3$/$+2.7$ at 2B; signs vary by seed) and near-parity on held-out.
At $k{=}16$ (tested at 0.8B) the vision port \emph{trails} ($-2.8$ and $-7.0$\,pt held-out,
$p\le 4\times10^{-3}$), with one seed clearly unstable. So the ``vision tokens are a better
landing pad'' hypothesis is \emph{not} supported at these scales --- but the ports demonstrably
learn different solutions (up to 1{,}800 discordant items per cell), the vision port received no
port-specific tuning, and vision pretraining strength grows sharply with model size in this
family. The port$\times$scale interaction at 27B+ remains open; the pilot's value is converting
it from speculation into a measured, non-trivial question, with all mechanics
(M-RoPE grid positions via the model's own indexer, KV-cached manual decoding) already built and
tested.

\subsection{Does it read the graph? Counterfactual faithfulness}
\label{sec:faithfulness}

\begin{table}[t]
  \caption{Causal graph probes (Qwen3-0.6B, 100k corpus, 400 single-fact probes per $k$, one
  seed). ``Swap-follow'': after rewiring the answer edge to a type-plausible false neighbor, the
  model outputs the \emph{false} answer --- the graph-reading signature; it scales with $k$.
  Ablating the answer edge collapses accuracy while ablating an unrelated edge does not: edges
  are encoded separably. The frozen base without concepts knows only 10\% of these probes.}
  \label{tab:faith}
  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{rccccc}
    \toprule
    $k$ & 1 & 4 & 8 & 16 & 32\\
    \midrule
    concept correct & 41\% & 56\% & 63\% & 65\% & 70\%\\
    ablate answer edge (want low) & 55\% & 38\% & 34\% & 36\% & 28\%\\
    ablate other edge (want high) & 90\% & 96\% & 92\% & 95\% & 94\%\\
    swap-follow $\to$ false answer & 0.8\% & 16.5\% & 25.8\% & 29.8\% & 34.2\%\\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

A compression method can succeed on QA while merely memorizing text patterns. Following the
deficiency diagnosed across graph-token systems \citep{gteval,sink}, we built causal probes in:
(a) \emph{edge ablation} --- removing the questioned edge should collapse that answer while
removing an unrelated edge should not; (b) \emph{counterfactual swap} --- rewiring the answer
edge to a plausible false neighbor should flip the model to the \emph{false} answer if and only
if it reads the graph rather than its parametric memory (the frozen base knows only 10\% of
these probes, so memory is controlled). Table~\ref{tab:faith}: edges are separably encoded at
every $k$, and swap-following rises monotonically from 0.8\% ($k{=}1$) to 34.2\% ($k{=}32$) ---
faithfulness is \emph{capacity-limited, not absent}, and becomes another axis that scale buys.
On the 10k-corpus Qwen3.5-0.8B pilots, swap-follow is much lower (5--13\%) --- confounded with
corpus size and training regime, but consistent with faithfulness being the slowest-developing
property. Off-topic behavior is preserved: on control tasks that mention the entity without
asking about its facts, injecting concepts moves the next-token distribution by a median KL of
only 0.06--0.08 nats at 0.6B/100k, flat in $k$ --- by construction (zero-initialized output
projection) the student starts bit-identical to the frozen model. The 10k-corpus pilots show
larger control-task perturbation ($\sim$0.27), another regime effect to track at scale.

\subsection{Optimization and stability notes}

For onboarding completeness: training stability was an early obstacle ($\sim$8\,pt outcome
spread between nominally identical runs). The stabilized recipe --- drop the multiplicative
tanh gate in favor of a zero-initialized output projection, effective batch 32 via gradient
accumulation, learning rate $10^{-4}$ held constant --- brought the spread to the $\sim$1\,pt
range \emph{as measured then}; the corrected protocol later showed much of the residual spread
was evaluation noise. Two honest caveats survive: our batch-size comparisons at fixed step
counts confound batch size with data passes (a larger effective batch sees more data before the
fixed stop), so we treat ``effective batch 32'' as a validated default rather than a mechanism
claim; and learning-rate rankings from short-horizon sweeps systematically favor small rates ---
we only trust horizon-matched comparisons.

\section{Open questions and planned experiments}
\label{sec:open}

\textbf{Q1 --- The data$\times$model interaction (central).} Does a larger frozen model simply
need more entities before injection pays, or is it intrinsically harder to steer? Planned: the
full surface --- entities $\{10\text{k}, 100\text{k}, 300\text{k}, 1\text{M}\}$ $\times$ Qwen3.5
$\{0.8, 2, 4, 9, 27\}$B dense, $k$-family, 3 seeds --- with the 100k$\times$\{9B, 27B\} corners
first, since they directly separate the ceiling from the steerability hypothesis. The 260k-entity
corpus is built; 1M is pipeline-ready. \emph{Status: not yet run; requires compute beyond our two
24\,GB GPUs for every backbone above $\sim$4B.}
\textbf{Q2 --- MoE: total or active parameters?} Qwen3.5's MoE members (35B-A3B, 122B-A10B,
397B-A17B) let us ask which parameter count governs injection. \emph{Not yet run.}
\textbf{Q3 --- Port$\times$scale.} Does the vision port's parity-at-$k{=}8$ become an advantage
at sizes with strong vision pretraining? \emph{Mechanics built; larger sizes not yet run.}
\textbf{Q4 --- Faithfulness at scale.} Does swap-following keep rising with data and $k$, and
can an auxiliary per-edge objective accelerate it? \emph{Design sketched, not yet run.}
\textbf{Q5 --- Missing baselines.} An xRAG-style retriever-vector bridge trained under our exact
protocol, per-entity prefix-tuning (the transductive upper baseline), and on-policy distillation
(GKD \citep{gkd}) as an objective ablation. \emph{Not yet run.}
\textbf{Q6 --- Beyond 1-hop.} 2-hop neighborhoods raise the facts-per-token ratio $\sim$10--100$\times$
at fixed $k$; we treat this as a rate--distortion ablation inside Q1, not a standalone
contribution (the per-question subgraph-encoding literature is crowded \citep{gnp,gretriever}).
\textbf{Q7 --- Second benchmark.} EntityQuestions integration for a second unseen-entity axis.
\emph{Loader stubbed.}

A compute-grant application (Swiss AI Initiative, CSCS Alps) is drafted to fund Q1--Q3; the
pilot surface (Figure~\ref{fig:surface}) is its preliminary-results centerpiece.

\section{Reproducibility and infrastructure}

Everything is public in the project repository: the data pipeline (snapshot, generation,
tiering, teacher-path extraction; all resumable and cached), training (single 24\,GB GPU per
run), and the evaluation harness. Every metric in this report traces to a W\&B run id (entity
\texttt{university-of-zurich}, project \texttt{conceptformer-v2}) or a re-runnable CLI command,
and every evaluation writes \emph{per-item} outputs so any two systems can be compared with
paired statistics after the fact. The finding-by-finding evidence log
(\texttt{docs/RESEARCH\_FINDINGS.md}, F1--F16 plus methods notes M1--M7) records not only
results but also the claims we \emph{retracted} and why --- start there, then
\texttt{docs/RELATED\_WORK.md} (annotated survey behind \S\ref{sec:related}) and
\texttt{docs/GRANT\_PROPOSAL\_DRAFT.md}. The three-command gate
(\texttt{ruff}, \texttt{ty}, \texttt{pytest}; 244 tests) is green at every commit.
