\section*{Proposal Description (max. 2 A4 pages)}

\subsection*{a. Purpose of the Swiss AI Initiative Small Project}
Large language models (LLMs) hold knowledge either \emph{in their weights} (uneditable, and
unreliably recalled for long-tail entities \cite{emptyshelves}) or \emph{as retrieved text}
(faithful but costing hundreds of context tokens per query). We develop a third channel:
compressing a knowledge-graph entity's neighborhood into $k\!\approx\!8$ continuous
\emph{concept tokens} consumed natively by a \emph{frozen} LLM --- attributable (each token
traces to citable Wikidata edges), editable, and $\sim$10$\times$ cheaper than text at equal
knowledge. Our published predecessor system (WWW Companion '26, \textbf{best paper award} of
its hosting workshop \cite{cfv1}) and extensive pilots establish that the approach works; the
project's purpose is to measure the \textbf{first scaling laws of knowledge injection}: how
injected-knowledge quality scales with (i) the amount of knowledge the encoder is trained on
and (ii) the size of the frozen model --- two axes our pilots show pull in \emph{opposite}
directions, whose unmeasured interaction decides whether this channel matters at frontier scale.

\subsection*{b. Current Situation of the Thematic Area}
Two literatures approach the problem. \emph{Soft-token compression} (gist tokens, ICAE, PISCO,
xRAG \cite{gist,icae,pisco,xrag}) compresses \emph{text} into continuous tokens; xRAG is
closest to us (frozen decoder, same-model distillation) but translates a single retriever
vector rather than learning compression, and evaluates neither unseen-entity generalization
nor faithfulness. \emph{Knowledge-graph injection} (GNP, GraphToken, KBLaM, Knowledge Prompts
\cite{gnp,graphtoken,kblam,knowledgeprompts}) encodes graphs per-question with labeled QA data,
or precomputes per-entity vectors as lookup tables that cannot handle unseen entities. 2026
meta-analyses find current graph-token systems are not faithful carriers of structure
\cite{gteval}. No existing work combines an \emph{amortized, inductive} per-entity encoder,
\emph{label-free} same-model distillation, and \emph{causal} faithfulness evaluation --- and,
decisively for this call: scaling laws exist for \emph{parametric} knowledge storage
\cite{physics33}, but \textbf{no study measures injection quality against frozen-model scale
within one model family, and no data-scaling law for a knowledge encoder exists.}

Our pilots (two consumer 24\,GB GPUs, $\sim$60 runs, all public) de-risk the measurement: at an
8-token budget, concept tokens beat question-aware retrieval and LLM-written summaries
2.6--3.2$\times$ on the full 14{,}266-question PopQA benchmark, an untrained-injection control
is near floor, counterfactual edge-swap probes show genuine graph reading, and injection
preserves off-topic behavior. Crucially, unseen-entity accuracy scales $2.05\times$ from 10k to
100k training entities \emph{without saturating}, while at fixed data the injection margin
\emph{halves} from 0.8B to 2B frozen parameters: the proposal funds completing a de-risked
measurement, not testing a new idea.

\subsection*{c. Activities}
All work runs on the \textbf{Qwen3.5 family} (dense 0.8B--27B; MoE 35B-A3B/122B-A10B; every
size natively multimodal), open Wikidata-derived corpora, and our public, CI-gated pipeline.
Compute is justified from a \emph{measured} anchor: one converged training run (0.6B backbone,
100k entities) takes 6.0 wall-hours on an RTX 4090 (public W\&B logs); assuming a conservative
2$\times$ realized GH200 speedup and a 1.4$\times$ convergence margin, a run costs
$4.2 \cdot P \cdot D$ GPU-h ($P$ = active-parameter ratio vs.\ 0.8B, $D$ = entity ratio vs.\
100k). Work packages (GPU-h): \textbf{WP0} corpus scale-up to 1M entities (240);
\textbf{WP1} data-scaling transect at 0.8B, $k$-family $\times$ 3 seeds $\times$ \{300k, 1M\}
entities (670); \textbf{WP2} dense model transect at 100k entities, 5 sizes $\times$ $k$-family
$\times$ 3 seeds + per-size teacher extraction, hyperparameter screens, and encoder-capacity
re-sweeps (4,870); \textbf{WP3} MoE: does injection track total or active parameters? (1,730,
incl.\ a 397B-A17B single-node-sharded stretch run); \textbf{WP4} the decisive
data$\times$model interaction corners, \{300k, 1M\} $\times$ \{9B, 27B\} (6,920); \textbf{WP5}
injection-port factor --- text-embedding vs.\ vision-token interface at 3 sizes (piloted:
parity at $k{=}8$; 1,300); \textbf{WP6} community baselines: xRAG-style bridge, per-entity
prefix-tuning, on-policy distillation, 2-hop rate--distortion (520); \textbf{WP7}
cross-cutting evaluation: full-benchmark PopQA + EntityQuestions + counterfactual faithfulness
and capability-preservation sweeps over $\sim$170 checkpoints (900). Subtotal 17,150; +25\%
contingency (restarts, cluster onboarding) $=$ 21,440; a named stretch tier (full $k$-family at
MoE/corners, a 3M-entity point, extra ports, convergence-verification) adds 5,570 $\Rightarrow$
\textbf{request 30,000 GPU-h}; a 20k fallback preserves both core laws. The workload is $\sim$200 independent single-node jobs; only
the two largest MoE models need intra-node sharding --- and every backbone above $\sim$4B is memory-infeasible on our own hardware, which
is the literal reason for this application. Storage: model weights, corpora, teacher caches,
$\sim$200 checkpoints, and per-item evaluation dumps total $\sim$4.5 TB; with cache headroom we
request 10 TB ($\sim$500k files).

\subsection*{e. Expected Outputs and Outcomes}
Within six months: (1) the \textbf{scaling-law paper} --- the first empirical laws for
knowledge injection into frozen LLMs, with the causal-faithfulness methodology; (2)
\textbf{open data}: Wikidata QA-distillation corpora at 100k/300k/1M entities with sha-manifested
snapshots (CC0 sources); (3) \textbf{open weights}: all $\sim$200 trained encoders on
HuggingFace; (4) \textbf{open source}: the full pipeline and evaluation harness (already
public, 244-test CI); (5) the \textbf{open results dataset}: every run's per-item outputs,
enabling third-party re-analysis without any GPU. Outcome-wise, the laws answer a
go/no-go question for the field --- \emph{is} injected knowledge a channel worth scaling? ---
and, if the answer is yes, directly seed a \textbf{Large Project proposal}: a
Swiss-made, provenance-carrying knowledge layer for open foundation models (e.g.\ Apertus-class
models), where the Small Project's recipe, corpora, and laws determine the design points worth
training at scale.

\subsection*{f. Importance for Switzerland, Europe, and beyond}
The project advances the Initiative's trustworthy-AI mission concretely: concept tokens make
model knowledge \emph{attributable} (every token traces to citable knowledge-graph edges, and
our counterfactual protocol \emph{proves} the model uses them), \emph{editable} (swap an edge,
the answer follows), and \emph{efficient} ($\sim$10$\times$ fewer context tokens than
retrieval at equal knowledge --- a direct inference-cost and energy lever).
For Switzerland and Europe, a knowledge channel that grounds
open models in curated, auditable sources rather than opaque weights is a building block for
digital sovereignty: public-sector and industry deployments can maintain their own knowledge
graphs and update model knowledge without retraining or per-query retrieval infrastructure ---
with every artifact open, the value chain stays reproducible and European.

\subsection*{g. Ethics and Regulatory Compliance}
Data: Wikidata (CC0) and public benchmarks; no personal data beyond encyclopedic facts about
public entities, no human subjects, no scraping. Generated training questions come from an
open-weights model and are released with their prompts; model weights used (Apache-2.0-family
licenses) permit research use and redistribution of derivatives, and all
released artifacts carry explicit licenses. The approach is transparency-\emph{enhancing}
(provenance-carrying knowledge aligns with EU AI Act expectations); we comply with UZH
research-integrity and data-management regulations and CSCS usage policies, and publish all
results, including negative ones.
